## Who is Kyle Poyar?

Kyle Poyar is one of the most respected voices in SaaS growth, pricing strategy, monetization, and product-led growth. Over the past 15 years, he has worked with software startups on GTM strategy, pricing & packaging, and revenue growth, helping companies scale from early traction to significant ARR milestones.

He is the founder and writer of Growth Unhinged, one of the leading newsletters covering SaaS growth, AI monetization, pricing strategy, and GTM trends. His work combines original research, benchmark data, operator interviews, and practical frameworks rather than opinion-driven content.

Previously, Kyle spent eight years as an Operating Partner at OpenView, where he worked closely with portfolio companies on scaling from early-stage growth through $100M+ ARR. Before OpenView, he was a Director at Simon-Kucher & Partners, one of the world's leading pricing strategy consultancies.

His writing consistently focuses on practical decisions founders and GTM leaders face rather than theoretical discussions.

# Insights from Recent LinkedIn Posts

## AI Transformation Is Messy

Key observations:

- AI pivots require leadership mandates.
- AI increases execution speed but not necessarily less work.
- Strong foundations (context, positioning, integrations, brand voice) matter more than ever.
- Product quality becomes more important than feature lists.
- Companies increasingly compete by educating buyers on evaluation criteria.

Key takeaway:

> Build around future AI capabilities instead of today's limitations while strengthening the strategic foundations underneath execution.

---

## Startups Become What They Hunt

Using research across more than 1,000 SaaS and AI companies, Kyle reinforces several observations:

- Initial customer choice has long-term consequences.
- Most companies never fundamentally change ICP.
- SMB-heavy businesses face significantly worse retention.
- Mid-market companies often achieve superior long-term growth.
- Pricing and customer selection shape company trajectory.

Key takeaway:

> Your earliest customer decisions create long-term strategic constraints that are difficult to reverse.

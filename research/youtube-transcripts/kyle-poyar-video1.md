# Core Philosophy

Kyle argues that AI is fundamentally changing software economics, forcing companies to rethink long-standing SaaS pricing models.

His central thesis is:

> Pricing is no longer a finance exercise—it's becoming one of the biggest strategic advantages for AI companies.

Unlike previous SaaS eras where companies could rely on flat subscriptions or seat-based pricing, AI introduces variable costs that require entirely new monetization frameworks.

Throughout the talk, he consistently emphasizes adaptability over certainty.

---

# Major Theme 1: The Old SaaS Pricing Playbook Is Breaking

Traditional SaaS followed a relatively predictable formula:

- Underprice initially
- Acquire customers
- Identify high-value segments
- Build additional functionality
- Increase pricing over time
- Repeat

That model worked well because software margins were extremely high.

AI changes this equation.

Large language models remain expensive.

Reasoning workloads increase costs.

Agentic workflows dramatically increase usage.

The highest-value customers often become the most expensive customers to serve.

Instead of software becoming infinitely cheaper, many AI businesses face rapidly increasing infrastructure costs.

Core insight:

> Growth without sustainable monetization becomes dangerous in AI-native businesses.

---

# Major Theme 2: Pricing Is Becoming More Dynamic

Kyle rejects the idea that there is one "best" pricing model.

Instead, companies should understand the advantages and tradeoffs of multiple structures.

Examples include:

- Flat subscriptions
- Seat-based pricing
- Usage pricing
- Hybrid pricing
- Credit systems
- Outcome-based pricing

Different customer segments may require different pricing architectures simultaneously.

Rather than simplifying pricing at all costs, companies should optimize pricing for customer value and business sustainability.

---

# Major Theme 3: Flat Fees Create Structural Problems

Flat subscriptions appear simple but introduce major long-term issues.

Problems include:

- Small customers paying too much
- Large customers paying too little
- No natural expansion path
- Unprofitable heavy users
- Poor alignment between value and revenue

Kyle encourages companies to introduce multiple monetization levers rather than relying on one fixed subscription.

Examples include:

- Product expansion
- Usage expansion
- Credits
- Premium capabilities
- Platform fees

Core lesson:

> Revenue should scale with customer value creation.

---

# Major Theme 4: Credits Are a Transitional Model

One of Kyle's strongest observations concerns credit-based pricing.

He sees credits becoming increasingly common across AI companies because they provide flexibility.

Advantages:

- Easy to monetize multiple actions
- Easy to launch new AI features
- Easier internal product monetization
- Better cost management
- Better alignment with usage

However, he does not believe credits represent the long-term destination.

Enterprise customers eventually struggle with managing dozens of independent credit systems across vendors.

Instead, Kyle frames credits as:

> A bridge toward value-based pricing.

---

# Major Theme 5: Outcome-Based Pricing Represents the Long-Term Direction

Kyle predicts increasing movement toward outcome-based monetization.

Rather than paying for:

- seats
- features
- tokens
- usage

customers increasingly pay for successful business outcomes.

Examples:

- Successful resolutions
- Revenue recovered
- Tasks completed
- Business value generated

However, he also warns that outcome pricing introduces new challenges:

- Attribution disputes
- Measurement complexity
- Predictability concerns
- Billing transparency
- Customer trust

Successful outcome pricing requires:

- consistent outcomes
- clear attribution
- measurable performance
- predictable economics

The concept is attractive but operationally difficult.

---

# Major Theme 6: Pricing Should Be Designed Before Products

Kyle repeatedly argues that monetization should not become an afterthought.

Instead:

Pricing → Product → Launch

not

Product → Launch → Figure out pricing later

Teams should think through monetization before engineering decisions lock them into inflexible architectures.

He also recommends:

- designing for future pricing changes
- preserving pricing optionality
- avoiding long-term rigid contracts
- preparing for continual iteration

Pricing flexibility becomes a competitive advantage.

---

# Major Theme 7: Every Company Needs Pricing Ownership

One recurring operational recommendation:

Someone must explicitly own pricing.

Ownership means:

- collecting customer feedback
- analyzing benchmarks
- measuring pricing KPIs
- reviewing experiments
- coordinating cross-functional input
- proposing pricing evolution

Kyle is less concerned with whether pricing sits under:

- Product
- Sales
- Finance
- Marketing
- Operations

and more concerned that somebody actively owns the process.

Without ownership, pricing becomes reactive instead of strategic.

# Key Takeaways

- AI fundamentally changes SaaS pricing economics.
- Flat subscriptions increasingly create structural problems.
- Hybrid and credit-based pricing are becoming mainstream.
- Credits are likely a bridge toward value-based monetization.
- Outcome-based pricing has enormous potential but significant operational complexity.
- Pricing should be designed alongside products rather than after launch.
- Companies should preserve pricing flexibility because AI markets evolve rapidly.
- Every software business should assign clear ownership of pricing strategy.
- Sustainable monetization is becoming a competitive advantage in the AI era.
- Long-term winners will align pricing with customer value rather than legacy SaaS conventions.

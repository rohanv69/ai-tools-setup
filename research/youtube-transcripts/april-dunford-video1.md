# April Dunford - Positioning Show

Source:

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9pf_iEomjY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9pf_iEomjY)

## Core Ideas

### Positioning requires repetition

- Strong market positioning comes from saying the same thing consistently.

- Companies usually change messaging too early.

- Repetition is necessary before customers actually associate your brand with a position.

Key quote:

> You're usually sick of hearing your positioning long before customers have actually internalized it.

---

## Positioning should be tested through sales conversations

April argues against homepage A/B testing for positioning.

Better approach:

- Convert positioning into a sales pitch

- Use top sales reps

- Test with qualified prospects

- Observe:

  - confusion

  - excitement

  - objections

  - questions

- Iterate based on live customer feedback

---

## Sales pitch structure

Typical flow:

1. Market insight

2. Existing alternatives

3. Perfect world vision

4. Product introduction

5. Differentiated value

6. Proof

7. Objection handling

8. Call to action

---

## Messaging should come after positioning validation

Recommended process:

Positioning

→ Sales pitch

→ Customer testing

→ Messaging

→ Homepage

Not:

Positioning

→ Homepage

---

## Messaging document philosophy

Maintain one canonical messaging document containing:

- company descriptions

- taglines

- differentiated value

- feature explanations

- approved graphics

- customer quotes

- spokesperson bios

Everything else should derive from this document.

---

## Positioning maintenance

Review positioning whenever:

- competitors change significantly

- differentiated capabilities change

- market shifts materially

- customer perception changes

Suggested cadence:

- 6-month executive positioning review

Participants:

- CEO

- Head of Sales

- Head of Product

- Marketing

---

## Core Philosophy

- Positioning precedes messaging.

- Messaging should remain consistent.

- Don't change messaging simply because the internal team is tired of it.

- Market repetition creates brand association.

- Strong positioning requires discipline and long-term consistency.
